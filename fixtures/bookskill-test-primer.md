# BookSkill Test Primer

A short original work created solely as licensed test data for the BookSkill Desktop project. It is not a real book, and it must not be treated as a substitute for a reader's own library.

## Chapter 1. The Lantern Index Principle

[P1.1] The workshop in Harbor Lane kept a lantern index: every claim written on a card had to name the shelf, the page, and the paragraph from which it came.

[P1.2] The governing principle of this primer is stated here in full:

[P1.3] A knowledge card is valid only when every claim cites a paragraph identifier from the source document.

[P1.4] Readers may locate that principle in Chapter 1, in the paragraph that begins "A knowledge card is valid only". Downstream citation tests should resolve a highlight of that sentence to this chapter and paragraph, not to a neighboring line.

[P1.5] The lantern keepers also recorded a second locatable rule: If two passages disagree, the card must cite both passages and must not silently prefer one.

[P1.6] This chapter does not discuss any third-party book. It exists only to give BookSkill a stable, licensed passage to point at.

## Chapter 2. The Hollow Echo Counterexample

[P2.1] On the opposite wall the keepers hung a counterexample, so that importers could practice rejection as well as citation.

[P2.2] The hollow echo is this unsupported sentence: Cedar trees sing at noon in every harbor. That sentence is a deliberate counterexample. It is not supported by any other passage in this primer, and it must not be promoted into a knowledge card as if it were a cited fact.

[P2.3] A correct system behavior is as follows: when a reader asks for a source for "cedar trees sing at noon," the product may point to this counterexample paragraph in Chapter 2, and must label the claim as unsourced or contradicted, not as an extracted principle.

[P2.4] The keepers closed the primer with a locatable refusal rule: No card may be saved when its only support is a hollow echo.

[P2.5] End of the BookSkill Test Primer.
